01 · Overview

Redesigning Regional Navigation Across Twilio Console

As Twilio expanded into Europe, Data Residency became a critical requirement — all personally identifiable information processed through Twilio's products must be stored within European borders to meet GDPR obligations and reduce latency for end users.

The existing regional experience had been built on top of the legacy Console architecture. When Twilio committed to a full Console rebuild, that navigation model was no longer viable — the way regional products were surfaced and switched simply didn't fit the new architecture.

Rather than patch the broken experience, I led the team to use the Console migration as the opportunity to redesign the entire regional navigation model from scratch — with a scalable regional switcher that could work across every regional product in Twilio.

02 · The Problem

Three pain points that made the regional experience broken

The legacy regional experience had three compounding pain points that made it broken for enterprise customers:

Pain 01

Hidden regional setup — users missing it entirely

To access regional products, users first had to navigate to the product catalogue and manually pin each product they wanted to use regionally. This flow was completely hidden — there was no indication in the main Console that a setup step was required. As a result, users were missing the regional setup entirely, either assuming regional products were unavailable or contacting support after failing to find them.

Pain 02

Double navigation — products duplicated 3× in sidebar

Once pinned, the same products appeared three times in the sidebar — once per region (US1, AU1, IE1) — with no explanation of which was active for compliance or why they were duplicated.

Pain 03

Navigation incompatible with new Console

The entire tab-based regional switching pattern was architecturally tied to the legacy Console structure. It was not going to survive the migration — a full redesign was unavoidable.

Legacy — regional navigation walkthrough

The pinning flow was hidden in the product catalogue — users had no idea they needed to go there first. Once pinned, products appeared duplicated 3× in the sidebar with no explanation of the region structure or which was active for compliance. Regional badges existed only inside this catalogue, so in daily Console use there was no way to tell which products were regional without coming back here.

04 · Process

From learning the domain to leading the system

1

Discovery across the regional landscape

I was new to the regional space at Twilio. Before designing anything, I ran discovery sessions with PMs and engineers across all regional product teams to understand each product's needs, existing pain points, and technical constraints. This gave me the full picture of the problem space.

2

Building the roadmap before building the design

The first deliverable wasn't a screen — it was a phased plan. I defined what we could deliver for Signal (Twilio's annual conference) as an MVP and what would follow. Aligning stakeholders on scope before design direction was set prevented expensive pivots later.

3

Designing the regional switcher

The core decision: replace the double-navigation model with a single header-level switcher — visible only on regional products, invisible on global ones. This made the regional/global distinction immediately legible without adding cognitive overhead for users who didn't need it.

4

Building the regional component library

Designed a rules-based component system for all Twilio product teams. Components carried their own logic — correct copy, states, and edge cases included. Teams switched them on or off; no room for misinterpretation. This was the leverage point that made company-wide rollout feasible.

5

Coordinating design across Twilio

Once patterns were established, I worked with every designer at Twilio who owned a regional product — reviewing work, resolving edge cases, and making judgment calls on pattern application. For Phone Numbers, Messaging, and Voice, I designed the new experiences directly.

Discovery — Twilio Regional Experience

The discovery synthesis I ran across every regional product team — mapping pain points, opportunities, and open questions before any design work began.

05 · Key design decisions

The tradeoffs that defined the system

Per-product region selectors

Keep region switching inside each product's settings. Familiar, easier to scope per team. Preserves existing mental model.

vs
Header-level regional switcher ✓

Single switcher at header level, only on regional products. One consistent model across all surfaces — no double navigation.

Always-visible region indicator

Show regional switcher on every Console page regardless. Full consistency, simpler engineering.

vs
Regional-only visibility ✓

Show the switcher only on regional products. The UI teaches the system model — users learn which products are regional through normal use.

Custom regional UI per product

Let each product team build their own regional patterns. Maximum flexibility, easier for individual teams to ship independently.

vs
Shared dynamic component library ✓

One rules-based system for all teams. No re-invention, no inconsistency, no room for error. Teams switch components on or off.

07 · Component system

Dynamic, rules-based components
any team could use

The component library was the leverage point of the entire initiative. Without it, 10+ product teams would have built their own regional patterns — inconsistent, error-prone, impossible to maintain.

I designed the library to be rules-based: components carried their own logic, copy, and edge case handling. Teams didn't design regional interactions — they switched the right component on and the correct behaviour came with it.

01
Single-select regional switcher

Used on all regional pages outside Insights. Users switch to any available region; unavailable regions are grayed out. Used on Messaging Services, Phone Numbers, Voice, and more.

02
Multi-select switcher

Used exclusively on Insights pages (Calls and Messaging). Allows filtering data across multiple regions simultaneously. Different interaction model, same visual language.

03
Disabled switcher state

For configuration pages where the product's region is already predetermined. Communicates "this is already regionalised" without allowing re-configuration at this level.

04
Region picker component

For product creation flows (e.g. new API Key, new Phone Number). Allows users to assign a region to a new resource at creation time, not after the fact.

Component guidelines — One Console Regional

The guidelines document I authored for all Twilio product teams — covering what Data Residency means, which regions are supported, what each component variant does, and explicit rules for what not to do. Copy ownership and edge case logic included. This document enabled 10+ teams to implement independently without breaking consistency.

09 · Reflections

What I learned leading a company-wide system

↺
Start with discovery even under shipping pressure

Coming in new to the regional domain, the temptation was to move fast. Taking time to run discovery across all product teams surfaced constraints and edge cases that would have caused expensive rework later. It was the right call.

↺
A shared decision log would have saved time on coordination

With 10+ teams involved, pattern decisions got revisited every time a new stakeholder joined a conversation. A living decision log from the start would have shortened every alignment call significantly.

✓
Building the component library early was the right investment

The upfront effort to build a rules-based, error-proof component system paid off enormously during rollout. Teams adopted it without friction — they didn't make design decisions, they applied the system.

✓
The UI should teach the system model

Making the switcher appear only on regional products was a small decision with big clarity gains. Users learned which products were regional through normal product use — no documentation required.
